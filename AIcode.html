AI Is the Construction Worker.
You Are Still the Architect.

A practitioner's view on where AI-assisted coding actually belongs in a serious workflow

There is no shortage of content about using AI for coding. Yet much of it describes a style of working that feels foreign to anyone with a structured approach to software development. This piece is an attempt to set that straight.

Coding is architecture first

Coding is fundamentally about structure, and about the choices you make when constructing something that solves a well-defined task. Before writing a line of implementation, you need a template — a cookiecutter repository that enforces the blueprint of the project. From there you choose your primary classes: a pytorch_model, a dataset, a trainer. These are the rooms. The shared utilities in utils.py are the electrical wiring.

Whether to build a class hierarchy for feed-forward and recurrent models or handle the distinction with a simple boolean flag is a design decision that belongs to the architect. It is not a question for AI.

What AI is actually good for

Think of AI as a skilled, tireless construction worker. On command, it can produce Hydra configuration files for hyperparameter sweeps, write Slurm scripts for matrix jobs, turn a day's commits into polished documentation, and generate HTML to make notebook results publication-ready. It can survey the landscape of algorithms for a task and write comprehensive tests for every case you have designed.

The right use of AI is not to critique your decisions. It is to execute them faithfully, at a speed and scale you could not match alone.

The distinction that matters

The common failure mode in AI-assisted development is treating the tool as a decision-maker rather than an implementer. The moment you outsource design choices to a language model, you lose the coherence that makes software maintainable. Keep the design decisions where they belong, and AI becomes genuinely powerful.
